How to Get an H-2B Work Placement from Jamaica (Without Getting Scammed)

A message says you’ve got an H-2B job in the United States. Then comes the catch: they want a deposit today, or a copy of your passport.

You want this badly. They know that. Before you send anything, stop.

5 min readUpdated November 4, 2026

By Laura Clarke

Lead Administrative Concierge

Don’t pay or send your passport until the Ministry of Labour and Social Security confirms the agency is licensed for H-2B. And no one can guarantee you a visa.

We do not recruit or place workers. We cannot get you a job or a visa, and nobody can guarantee one. This guide helps you stay safe, and we help with the Jamaican documents you will need.

Step one, before anything else

Check the agency with the Ministry

Recruiting workers for overseas jobs without an active registration with the Ministry of Labour and Social Security is against Jamaican law. The Ministry’s Employment Agencies Unit publishes a list of approved overseas job placement agencies. In September 2026 it named about 80 agencies across the island, and it shows which programmes each one is approved for. Check that yours is approved for H-2B, not only for another programme such as J-1. The list changes monthly, so always check the latest one.

Ask: “Is this agency currently licensed for H-2B?” Write down the date and who you spoke to. You are not limited to an agency in your parish.

The Embassy checks your recruiter too

Since January 1, 2025, H-2B applicants must name their recruiting agency on the visa application. The U.S. Embassy checks it against the Ministry’s register or the U.S. Department of Labor’s recruiter list. If your recruiter is not registered, the application is rejected and you must apply again through a registered one.

Protect your money

If someone is already asking you for something

The U.S. Embassy in Kingston has warned that unregistered recruiters have a history of charging illegal fees and placing Jamaican workers at different worksites from the ones on their applications. Here is what to do in the three most common situations.

If they asked for money

Under U.S. rules, H-2B employers and their recruiters may not charge workers a recruitment or placement fee. If an agency asks for any payment, ask the Employment Agencies Unit whether that fee is allowed before you pay, and always get a receipt. Cash with no receipt, payments by WhatsApp or mobile money, and pressure to pay today are all red flags. A “guaranteed” visa or a paid “fast-track” is the biggest one.

If they asked for your passport

Keep clear photos or scans on your phone, but do not send passport copies to anyone until you have confirmed they are licensed. Anyone asking for your passport before anything has been checked is a reason to walk away.

If they found you on social media

Jobs offered only on TikTok, Instagram or Facebook, with no named employer, no written contract or job offer, and no licence number the Ministry can confirm are scams until proven otherwise. The Ministry does not recruit through TikTok or Instagram, and pages that use the Minister’s photo to offer jobs are scams.

Be honest on every form

Lying or taking part in fraud can make you permanently ineligible for a U.S. visa. Your visa application also asks who recruited you, so the Embassy will see who you used.

Why no one can promise you

The visa limit, and why timing matters

H-2B is a U.S. visa for temporary, seasonal, non-farm work, such as hotels, housekeeping, kitchens and landscaping. You do not have to be a student. A U.S. employer applies for approval first, and only after that is approved do you attend a visa interview at the U.S. Embassy in Kingston.

The U.S. issues only 66,000 H-2B visas a year, split between two halves of the year, but demand usually beats the limit. In 2026 the limit for jobs starting from April was reached on March 10. Extra visas were then released, with some reserved for workers who had been before. Therefore, no agency can guarantee you a visa, whatever they say.

Timing matters. Employers typically file in early January for jobs starting in April, so October to December is the time to get your documents ready and make your agency calls. Dates shift each year, so confirm the current position on the USCIS website.

One more option

The Ministry’s own programme

The Ministry of Labour and Social Security also runs its own Overseas Employment Programme. The Minister has said plainly that there is no fee at any stage of the Ministry’s recruitment or placement. If someone using the Ministry’s name asks you for any payment other than the visa fee at the end of the process, they are not authorised.

Ask your Member of Parliament’s constituency office how selection works in your area, or contact the Ministry directly through mlss.gov.jm. It costs nothing to ask, and it gives you one more chance alongside the agencies.
