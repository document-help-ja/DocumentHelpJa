First, check your purpose

Is this the right visa for your visit?

B-1 covers certain temporary business visits; B-2 covers tourism, family visits and other permitted visitor activities. They are often combined as B1/B2.

Visitor purposes

A temporary visit

A holiday, visiting relatives or friends, or attending a business meeting can fit a visitor visa.

Different purpose

Work, study or relocation

A visitor visa does not authorise employment or living permanently in the United States. Other visa categories have their own requirements.

Visa validity is different from your permitted stay. A visa lets you travel to request entry. U.S. border officials decide admission and the length of each stay.

The broad picture

What does the application involve?

  1. Prepare and review your application

    The DS-160 records your personal details, travel purpose and background. Accurate, complete answers matter.

  2. Pay and arrange the next stage

    The government application fee and appointment arrangements follow the official process for Jamaica. Eligible renewals may receive different instructions.

  3. Attend your interview, if required

    Answer the officer’s questions honestly and bring the documents requested in your appointment instructions.

  4. Follow the decision instructions

    You may receive an approval, a refusal, or instructions for additional documents or processing. Follow the specific instructions given to you.

What is required from you?

Assistance can make the paperwork easier to manage. You still provide the facts, review your answers and take part in the application yourself.

  • Give truthful, complete information. Mention earlier refusals, travel issues and relevant history. Ask if you are unsure how a question applies.
  • Review before signing. Make sure the form reflects your circumstances, and that any preparer is identified where required.
  • Keep your records. Retain your confirmation pages, payment receipt and appointment instructions.
  • Attend when required. Prepare your passport, DS-160 confirmation, appointment confirmation and any other requested documents.

Check the Embassy’s current appointment instructions before your visit, including security rules. Requirements for what you may bring can change.

Already held a U.S. visa?

Renewal is still a new application

Keep your old visa and passport available when preparing. A previous visa does not guarantee another one, and renewal does not automatically mean you can skip the interview.

Some applicants may qualify for an interview waiver under current rules. Eligibility depends on the previous visa and other conditions; the Embassy can still require an interview.

Before you decide

Common questions

Does an invitation letter guarantee a visa?

No. A letter of invitation is not required for a visitor visa, and it does not guarantee approval. Your own circumstances and eligibility matter.

Should I buy my ticket before applying?

Wait until you have your visa before making final travel plans or buying tickets. An appointment, payment or interview does not guarantee issuance.

What happens if I am refused?

Read the refusal notice and follow the instructions given. Refusal reasons differ. For a section 214(b) refusal, there is no appeal; applying again requires a new application and fee. Consider what has changed before reapplying. For a legal eligibility issue, seek advice from a qualified U.S. immigration attorney.

Can someone help me with my application?

Yes. You can receive help preparing the application, but the information must come from you. Any preparer must be disclosed where the form asks. Review all answers before signing. Adult applicants generally sign their own application; different rules can apply for children or applicants unable to sign.

Can I work in the United States on this visa?

A visitor visa does not authorise employment in the United States. If work is the purpose of your trip, you need to consider the appropriate visa category.
