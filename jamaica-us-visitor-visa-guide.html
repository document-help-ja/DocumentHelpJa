U.S. Visitor Visa from Jamaica

A relative says come for the holidays. You open the application, see how long it is, and see a fee you won’t get back if it goes wrong.

Most people don’t fail because of the paperwork. They fail because they rushed in. Here’s what to know first.

By Laura ClarkeUpdated November 4, 2026

Apply once, and apply prepared. The fee is non-refundable, a refusal means starting over with a new fee, and you shouldn’t buy a ticket until you have your visa.

This guide helps you decide and avoid costly mistakes. It does not walk through the application step by step. That is what we do with you, so your answers are accurate and complete. We are not the U.S. Embassy, and nobody can guarantee approval.

At a glance

What you’re really applying for

Whether you hope to visit family, take a holiday or attend a business meeting, a visitor visa is a request to travel to the United States for a short, temporary stay. The application records your personal details, your travel purpose and your background. Good preparation helps you present that information clearly. It does not guarantee a visa. Decisions are made by the U.S. Embassy, independently of any help you use.

First, check your purpose

Is this the right visa for your visit?

B-1 covers certain temporary business visits. B-2 covers tourism, family visits and other permitted visitor activities. They are often combined as B1/B2.

A temporary visit

A holiday, visiting relatives or friends, or attending a business meeting can fit a visitor visa.

Work, study or relocation

A visitor visa does not authorise employment or living permanently in the United States. Other visa categories have their own requirements.

Visa validity is different from your permitted stay. A visa lets you travel to request entry. U.S. border officials decide admission and the length of each stay.
Even when someone assists you

What only you can do

Assistance can make the paperwork easier to manage. You still provide the facts, review your answers and take part in the application yourself. That is where most costly mistakes happen.

  • Give truthful, complete information. Mention earlier refusals, travel issues and relevant history. Ask if you are unsure how a question applies.
  • Review before signing. Make sure the form reflects your circumstances, and that any preparer is identified where required.
  • Keep your records. Retain your confirmation pages, payment receipt and appointment instructions.
  • Attend when required, and follow the Embassy’s current appointment instructions, including security rules.

Be cautious of guaranteed approvals, invented information or claims of special Embassy access. Expect a clear service description, separate government and service fees, and a chance to review your answers.

There is no fixed completion date. Appointment availability, additional processing and passport return can affect timing. Therefore, apply early, and avoid buying tickets or making final travel plans until you have your visa.

Already held a U.S. visa?

Renewal is still a new application. Keep your old visa and passport available. A previous visa does not guarantee another one, and renewal does not automatically mean you can skip the interview. Some applicants may qualify for an interview waiver under current rules, but eligibility depends on the previous visa and other conditions, and the Embassy can still require an interview. Before you decide

Common questions

Does an invitation letter guarantee a visa?

No. A letter of invitation is not required for a visitor visa, and it does not guarantee approval. Your own circumstances and eligibility matter.

Should I buy my ticket before applying?

Wait until you have your visa before making final travel plans or buying tickets. An appointment, payment or interview does not guarantee issuance.

What happens if I am refused?

Read the refusal notice and follow the instructions given. Refusal reasons differ. For a section 214(b) refusal, there is no appeal; applying again requires a new application and fee. Consider what has changed before reapplying. For a legal eligibility issue, seek advice from a qualified U.S. immigration attorney.

Can someone help me with my application?

Yes. You can receive help preparing the application, but the information must come from you. Any preparer must be disclosed where the form asks. Review all answers before signing. Adult applicants generally sign their own application; different rules can apply for children or applicants unable to sign.

Can I work in the United States on this visa?

A visitor visa does not authorise employment in the United States. If work is the purpose of your trip, you need to consider the appropriate visa category.
