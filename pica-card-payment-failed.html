PASSPORT RENEWAL · PAYMENT HELP

PICA payment failed?
Here’s what to check.

You filled in every box. You hit pay. Then: declined.

Now the application won’t reopen, and your bank says money left your account, but PICA hasn’t confirmed a thing. Your stomach drops.

Take a breath. This happens more than you’d think, and it’s usually fixable. The one thing to avoid: don’t pay again yet.

By Laura Clarke · Laura Clarke Administrative Services

Why your card keeps failing on the PICA portal

First, hold on to what you have

Before you touch anything else, save your application reference, any receipt or confirmation, the date and time you tried to pay, and a screenshot of the exact message. That screenshot helps whoever looks into it. Keep the original private, and hide your card and personal details if you ever share it outside an official secure support channel.

Your bank can explain a declined payment or a payment showing in your account. PICA can check your application and payment in its system. If access is blocked or a payment is unclear, ask PICA how to continue before you start a new application or pay again.

One thing that has worked

Paying with a Jamaican card has worked in my experience.

Now, which of these sounds like you?

If your card was declined

Ask your bank why the payment failed and whether you need to do anything. PICA sends questions about unsuccessful payments to your bank, so that’s the first call to make.

If your application is locked

Here’s what we’ve seen. After a failed payment, the application can lock for about two hours. Clicking retry doesn’t speed it up, so step away and come back.

Still locked? Contact PICA, give them the exact error message, and ask how to continue. A declined card alone doesn’t explain why you’re blocked, so the message matters. PICA handles access to applications. My service covers completing your online renewal and paying the fee.

If you started more than five business days ago and couldn’t finish, or couldn’t email the application for printing, PICA’s Resume option may help, or you can call customer care. Keep your existing reference and ask whether you can resume or need another route.

If your bank shows a payment

Money leaving your account isn’t the same as PICA confirming it. Check your email, spam included, and ask your bank whether the payment is pending or completed. Then send PICA your application reference and payment details through its official support channel, and ask them to check before you pay again.

If you see two payments, report it promptly. PICA considers payment errors and refunds under its policy. An error message doesn’t guarantee a refund, or when a payment will be reversed.

Keep your payment details private.

Never share your full card number, security code, PIN, banking password or one-time banking code in a public message. Don’t put them in our enquiry form either.

If online just won’t work

You can finish the process in person at a PICA office in Jamaica or a Jamaican overseas mission. PICA lists what to bring: your printed application, your current passport, two professionally produced passport photographs, and the applicable fees.

Call first. Ask how they’ll handle your existing application, whether you need an appointment or extra documents, and mention any payment your bank shows that PICA hasn’t confirmed.

Tell them your application reference, the exact error message, and when it happened. Follow their instructions if they need more information.

Whichever situation you’re in, the rule is the same: don’t pay twice until PICA confirms.
