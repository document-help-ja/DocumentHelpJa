Here is how to file the free digital C5 form, what you can bring duty-free, the two-month window for goods you ship separately, and exactly what happens at the wharf, all checked against Jamaica Customs’ own guidance.

Before you fly

The C5 Passenger Declaration

Since September 1, 2023, the paper C5 card has been replaced by the Electronic Passenger Declaration. Every passenger arriving at Jamaica’s international airports submits it online before landing.

  1. Open the official portal only

    Go directly to enterjamaica.gov.jm (the Jamaica Customs website links to it too). The form is free for visitors and residents.

  2. Declare everything accurately

    Answer every question truthfully, including what you are bringing and any goods you have shipped separately.

  3. Keep your confirmation

    Save or screenshot your confirmation QR code and have it ready when you arrive.

Never pay for the C5

Some websites charge card fees or promise “expedited processing”. They are not the government portal. The official C5 is always free.

What’s free of duty

Your duty-free allowance

Jamaica Customs allows passengers to bring certain goods in without paying duty, as long as they are not for sale:

  • Personal and household effects, including gifts: up to a combined value of US$1,000 (or the Jamaican equivalent), for passengers aged 18 and over.
  • Used household effects: items you have used for a reasonable period and are not importing to sell.
  • Tools of your trade: instruments and tools you already own and actively use for your profession or job.
  • Alcohol, tobacco and perfume: 2 litres of alcohol; 200 cigarettes, 50 cigars or 230 g of tobacco; 170 ml of perfume and 340 ml of eau de toilette.
With you on the plane

Accompanied baggage

Suitcases, carry-ons and personal items on the same flight as you. These are assessed at the airport.

Shipped separately

Unaccompanied baggage

Barrels, crates or boxes sent by sea or air freight. These are cleared later at the wharf or bonded warehouse.

Barrels you shipped

The Unaccompanied Baggage Allowance (UBA)

Goods you ship separately are covered by the Unaccompanied Baggage Allowance. Jamaica Customs offers a UBA approval form as an online e-service.

Watch the clock: Jamaica Customs says your UBA is valid for only two months after your goods arrive in Jamaica. Clearing after that can mean delays or extra charges, on top of the storage fees that build up while barrels sit at the port.

The 5 steps at the warehouse

  1. Go to the Public Bonded warehouse

    After collecting your shipping documents and paying the agent’s handling or storage fees.

  2. Show your ID and TRN

    Present them to the Customs staff.

  3. Find your shipment and wait for inspection

    Show your shipping documents to security, then wait for the Customs officer’s instructions to open your barrel for examination.

  4. Pay at the Customs cashier

    After the examination, the officer directs you to the cashier.

  5. Collect your release and gate pass

    You receive a Release Order, which you exchange with the warehouse operator for a Gate Pass.

  • Personal effects or non-commercial barrelJ$6,500flat rate per barrel, subject to change
  • Commercial or dutiable goodsAssessedduty worked out item by item

You unpack and repack it yourself

Section 227 of the Customs Act requires you to unpack and repack your own shipment. Plan your time, and pack so the contents are easy to take out and put back.

Can’t be there?

Having someone clear your barrels for you

A family member or friend can clear your barrels if they bring:

  • An authorisation letter from you, signed and stamped by a Justice of the Peace (JP).
  • Your TRN and your ID (the importer’s). Any photocopies must be signed and stamped by a JP.
  • The shipping documents for the barrels.

Avoid these

Costly mistakes to avoid

Paying a look-alike C5 website

The official C5 is free on enterjamaica.gov.jm. Any site demanding a card payment or “expedition fee” is not the government portal.

Leaving barrels at the port too long

Your UBA is valid for two months after your goods arrive, and storage fees keep adding up. Clear your shipment as soon as you can.

Packing items for sale in a personal barrel

The J$6,500 flat rate only covers personal effects and non-commercial items. Goods for sale are assessed for duty separately.

Requirements and fees checked against Jamaica Customs Agency guidance in September 2026. Rules and fees can change without notice, so confirm on jca.gov.jm or call Jamaica Customs on 876-922-5140 before you ship.
