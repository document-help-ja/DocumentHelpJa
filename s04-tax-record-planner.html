Why this planner

The receipt bag

March comes, and the receipts arrive in a bag. Job pay sits mixed in with business money. A client’s fees get counted as income. Cash sales have nothing written down.

The TAJ portal does the sums for you. So when one figure lands in the wrong place, everything after it is wrong too, and nobody notices until the bill comes.

“I put one figure in the wrong box on the portal, and I paid tax I didn’t owe.”Laura Clarke

Enter it once. In the right box.

Each kind of money has its own page, and each figure shows the portal line it goes on. Job pay, client fees, equipment and everyday costs never get mixed up.

Fill it in with your client, face to face or over the phone. By March, you work down the pages and enter each figure in its box.

Proof for cash

Cash sales, market buys and small jobs often come with no receipt. The planner has pages to write them down the same day, with a column for what backs each one up: a till tape, a Lynk record, a supplier’s invoice book or a WhatsApp message.

Notes made on the day carry far more weight than ones pieced together in March.
