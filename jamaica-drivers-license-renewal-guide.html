Tickets, Demerit Points and Your Driver’s Licence

What the new points system means for your licence, and what to do about old tickets.

Your driving record

Your licence now keeps score

For years, paying a ticket felt like the end of it. Now it’s the other way round. Paying is what puts the points on your licence.

Paying a ticket doesn’t make it go away. Paying it is what puts the points on your licence.

Prefer to watch? Missed court, unpaid tickets and demerit points in one video.

If you pay a ticket

Every driver starts at zero. Points are added for certain traffic offences, but not when the police hand you the ticket. They’re added when you pay the fine, or when you’re convicted in court. Therefore, when points reach set levels, the licence is suspended.

10 to 13 pointsSuspended 6 months
14 to 19 pointsSuspended 1 year
20 or more pointsSuspended 2 years

The good news: points clear after 15 months, as long as your licence wasn’t suspended in that time.

If you collect a few small tickets

No seatbelt is 2 points. Using a phone while driving is 3. Two of each is 10 points, and that’s six months without your licence.

Where it stands right now

Correct as at 4 November 2026

The Government announced that the system would start on 1 October 2026. In August it began a review, and on 28 August the Transport Authority said the start was delayed until a full revision is done.

On 9 October, Minister Vaz said the demerit rules are law, but the Island Traffic Authority’s processes aren’t finished. The delay is to complete them, correct data, and decide the legal way to reset existing points.

The review is also looking at which offences should carry points, so the list may change. No new start date has been announced. The Minister has said there will be zero tolerance once the system is fully in place.

If a case goes to court

None of this replaces what a judge can do. The Chief Justice has said that courts continue to apply demerit points and disqualify drivers where the law requires, whatever the administrative system is doing.

If you’re waiting for an amnesty

In July, the Minister said that even if the Government considers an amnesty on outstanding ticket fines, it would not apply to suspension of a licence under the demerit system. Paying late is better than not paying, but it won’t make the points disappear.

If you’ve been putting off a ticket

Look first. The free lookup shows what’s outstanding on your licence, and it’s better to find out now than at the cashier line. Then deal with what you find while the points system is still waiting to start.

Nobody knows the new start date, which offences will count, or how existing points will be handled. What you do know is that outstanding tickets block your renewal, and a ticket left too long can end up in court, where it can’t be paid online.

Before the planned start, the Government urged drivers to pay outstanding tickets by 30 September. That date has passed, but the idea behind it hasn’t. Therefore, check now, settle what’s there, and you won’t be caught out when the system starts.

If a ticket is past its court date, it can’t be paid online. Contact one of these:

  • The parish court where the ticket was issued
  • Court Administration Division (cad.gov.jm) · customerservice@cad.gov.jm
  • JCF Public Safety and Traffic Enforcement Branch (jcf.gov.jm) · psteb@jcf.gov.jm

Keeping your record clean

The Government says the point of the system is safer roads: fewer violations, more accountability and lives saved. It has also said it remains committed to bringing the system in, so a clean record is worth building now.

The simplest habits are the ones most drivers already know. Seatbelt on. Phone down. Those are exactly the 2-point and 3-point tickets. And if you do get a ticket, don’t ignore it, because an outstanding ticket will block your renewal.

If you’re due to renew

A licence can’t be renewed while there are outstanding tickets, and the same goes for transferring a vehicle’s ownership. You can check yours for free at trafficticketlookup.gov.jm. Have your licence card in front of you.

If you’d rather not chase it, we can check your tickets for you.

Drive safely, keep your record clean, and remember: paying a ticket doesn’t make it go away.
