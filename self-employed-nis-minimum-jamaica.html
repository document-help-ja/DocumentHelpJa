THE SELF-EMPLOYED GUIDE · UNDERSTANDING NIS

Why Might NIS Still Be Payable?

Why low business income, or no income tax to pay, doesn’t settle the NIS question.

A hairdresser has had a quiet year. A vendor has spent most of the earnings on running costs. A barber has only just opened his shop. Each wants to know the same thing: if I earned so little, why might NIS still need attention?

Income tax and National Insurance Scheme (NIS) contributions follow different rules. This guide explains the minimum you may have heard about, and what to check for your own situation.

THE KEY POINT

Having no income tax to pay does not, by itself, settle your NIS position.

The minimum contribution, in plain numbers

NIS is a social insurance scheme. Contributions help you qualify for benefits, subject to the conditions for each one.

Self-employed people pay the full 6% themselves, up to the insurable earnings ceiling. Official information published in 2026 also describes a minimum contribution category of J$250 a week. As a simple illustration, J$250 for 52 weeks comes to J$13,000.

This is not an automatic bill for every reader.

It explains the figure you may have heard. It does not mean everyone owes another J$13,000 regardless of their circumstances. Confirm the year, category and period with NIS or TAJ before using it to decide what you owe.

Find your situation

Read the one that sounds like you.

“My return shows nil.”

A nil figure is not an instruction to ignore contributions. And “nil” is used loosely: no sales, no profit and no income tax payable are three different situations. Describe your actual position.

“I started late, stopped trading or made a loss.”

Explain the real circumstances when your position is checked. Don’t enter nil figures just because the business felt unprofitable, or because you weren’t sure what to enter. Keep evidence of when you started or stopped trading, and your income and expenses for the period.

“I also have a regular job.”

A teacher with a weekend business, or a salon owner who also earns a salary, needs records for both. Keep payslips, annual earnings and deduction certificates, business records and contribution receipts together.

Don’t assume your salary deductions cover everything, or that a full self-employed minimum is automatically added on top. Ask for the combined position to be checked, including contributions already paid.

“There’s a balance I didn’t expect.”

Before drawing conclusions, identify the year, the contribution type, the amount and any payments already made. Ask whether it relates to an estimate, an annual return, a payment that needs tracing or another adjustment. Don’t pay twice just because a receipt is hard to find, and don’t ignore a figure because you didn’t expect it. Get it explained.

Filed is not the same as paid
