THE SELF-EMPLOYED GUIDE · GETTING BACK ON TRACK

Behind on Your S04 Returns or Payments? Where to Start

Six practical steps towards a clearer position.

A vendor has kept working but fallen behind with the paperwork. A hairdresser opened a TAJ online account and thought that meant she had filed. A barber needs a Tax Compliance Certificate and finds that earlier returns need attention.

If that sounds like you, there is a sensible place to start. Not every step will apply to you; skip the ones that don’t.

By Laura Clarke · Laura Clarke Administrative Services

A guide to the tasks, not a fixed order for every backlog. Follow the deadlines that apply to you.

Have a notice with a deadline? Read it first.

Don’t wait until every receipt is sorted. Check the notice and its dates now, then see Step 3. Your deadlines and TAJ’s directions set the order of work.

Step 1

Gather what you have

Bring these together so you can see the years involved, what has been done and what needs checking.

The years involved, and when your business started or stopped
Shows which periods to check.
Filed returns and submission confirmations
Shows what has already been reported.
Payment receipts and references
Helps trace payments and avoid paying twice.
Business income, expenses and employment records
Supports preparing or correcting figures.
Notices and letters from TAJ
Shows deadlines and questions needing a reply.
Step 2

Find out what is outstanding

An account is not a return. Creating a TAJ online account gives you access; it does not submit your income for you. An empty screen does not mean you are up to date. Check which returns apply to you and whether each was actually submitted. Keep the filing acknowledgement, not just a saved form.

If a balance is unclear, ask TAJ to explain it by period and tax type.

Step 3

Deal with anything that has a deadline

If TAJ has sent you a Notice of Assessment, read it and its dates straight away. Don’t leave it until the next filing season.

TAJ’s guidance says an objection must be filed within 30 days of the date the notice was served. Since July 1, 2025, objections must be filed online, with a signed objection letter uploaded to your TAJ account.

Filing a missing return does not automatically cancel an assessment or replace an objection. Get help suited to the notice, and keep evidence that supports your position.

Step 4

File what is missing, and check it first

Check your figures against your records before you submit. The system accepting a return does not mean it is correct. Someone with a salary and a side business may remember the business figures and leave out the employment income. Someone else may enter a guess just to get through the form.

If your records or figures need professional review, arrange that before entering them. One approach does not fit every backlog; follow your actual deadlines and TAJ’s directions.

If an earlier return is wrong, find out the correction process. Keep the original, your records and any amendment acknowledgement. Changing one figure does not automatically fix every later balance.

Step 5

Pay, or talk to TAJ about paying

Submitting a return does not make the payment, and paying does not submit the return.

Keep both pieces of evidence

Filing confirmation

Shows your return was submitted.

Payment receipt

Shows the payment was made.

Check the match: correct tax type, correct period and correct payment reference. A saved draft is not proof of filing, and a payment receipt is not proof that a return was filed.

Late filing and late payment can each bring penalties and interest. If you can’t pay in full, speak to TAJ rather than leaving the return unfiled. Don’t assume a payment arrangement or waiver has been granted until TAJ confirms it.

Step 6

Stay up to date from here

Your S04A sets out the estimated payments for the year you are in. Make a reasonable estimate based on your records, and if your trading changes a lot, ask TAJ or a tax professional whether to revise it. The quarterly calendar in our first guide shows the usual dates.
