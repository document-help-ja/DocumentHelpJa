NIS RECORD GUIDE · FOR EMPLOYEES AND THE SELF-EMPLOYED

Are Your NIS Contributions on Record?

What to check when you have changed jobs, changed your name or started working for yourself.

A hotel worker has moved between employers. A hairdresser left a salaried job to open a salon. Someone has married and now uses a different surname from the one on an older NIS card.

Each has a reason to check that their contribution record matches their working history. Checking now is easier than at retirement, while the paperwork is still easy to find.

THE KEY POINT

Years spent working are not, on their own, proof of contributions.

Why your NIS record matters

Your contribution history helps decide which benefits you qualify for. Retirement is one part of the scheme; other benefits have their own conditions.

A payslip showing an NIS deduction is useful evidence, but check the record NIS holds as well.

In June 2026, NIS Director Portia Magnus urged contributors to check their records regularly and to report former employers who have not paid in their contributions.

Find your situation

Contact NIS at a Ministry of Labour and Social Security parish office. Explain why you are asking, and ask how to get or review your contribution record and what to bring.

You have worked for several employers

Ask NIS

“Are all the periods and employers on my record?”

A period seems to be missing

Ask NIS

“What information would help you investigate it?”

Your name or other details changed

Ask NIS

“What do you need to update my record and link the earlier details?”

You moved from a job into your own business

Ask NIS

“How do I check contributions across both periods?”

You stopped working or moved overseas

Ask NIS

“Do voluntary contributions or another arrangement apply to me?”

You don’t need to know the cause before you ask. Describe what you can see and what you expected to find.

Gather a working folder

Have your NIS number and ID ready. Depending on your question, these may help:

Your NIS enquiry folder

  • Working historyEmployer detailsNames, locations and approximate dates.
  • Employment evidencePayslips and certificatesAnnual earnings certificates, such as the P24.
  • Self-employed evidenceReturns and receiptsSelf-employed returns, payment receipts and stamp-card surrender receipts.
  • Linking your detailsName-change documentsProof of a name change and copies of earlier letters.

A preparation list, not an official checklist. Confirm what the office needs before you go. Keep a copy of what you hand in, and note the date and reason for your enquiry.

If contributions seem to be missing

First identify the employer and period. Compare your records with what NIS provides, and ask whether the cause is employer reporting, a payment that needs tracing, incorrect personal details or something else.

A missing entry does not prove an employer kept your money, or that the contribution is lost for good. If the business has closed or you can’t find every payslip, say so; it is still worth asking.

If you have changed your name

Check that your employer has your correct NIS number, and ask NIS how to update its own records. Updating one organisation does not update the others. Keep documents linking your previous and current names, and ask NIS to look at your actual record.

Can you keep contributing after leaving work?

Voluntary contribution needs an application and approval; it is not simply paying any amount in. Your work status, age, contribution history and time overseas may matter. Ask NIS whether it applies to you, and how and for which period to pay. Don’t assume buying stamps now repairs every past gap; check first.

What about a pension or grant?

NIS decides based on your contribution record and the conditions for each benefit. A pension and a one-time grant are different outcomes, and this guide can’t tell you which you qualify for. If you are nearing retirement, ask NIS about your eligibility and anything that needs investigating. If a family member has died, ask promptly about benefits and claim deadlines.

How this connects with your business

If you work for yourself, keep your return confirmations and contribution receipts with your business records. Read Working for Yourself in Jamaica? and Why might NIS still be payable? for the link to filing.
