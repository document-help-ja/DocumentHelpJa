Lost, Stolen, or Damaged Jamaican Passport? Why You Can't Just Fill Out a Standard Renewal

We’ve all experienced that split second of panic. You are packing for a trip, moving to a new apartment, or maybe just doing a deep clean, and you suddenly realize your Jamaican passport is missing. Or worse, you pull it out of a pocket only to discover it went through a full wash and spin cycle in the laundry.

Your first instinct might be to jump onto the Passport, Immigration and Citizenship Agency (PICA) website and apply for a quick online renewal. But stop right there. If you cannot present your valid or expired physical passport in pristine condition, you are no longer doing a standard renewal. You are now officially applying to replace a lost, stolen, or damaged passport—and the process is entirely different.

The Security Trap: Why Standard Forms Get Rejected

To PICA, a missing or damaged passport isn't just an inconvenience; it’s a high-priority national security issue. Your passport is a powerful identity document. When it falls into the wrong hands, it poses a risk of identity theft and fraud. PICA takes this very seriously.

If you attempt to submit a standard passport renewal form and simply leave the "previous passport number" blank, or if you try to use the online portal which requires your physical document, you will face an immediate counter rejection. A replacement application requires paper-based filing, specific legal declarations, and a mandatory investigation by PICA’s Investigation and Surveillance Unit before they issue a new booklet.

Lost or Stolen? You Must Provide a Police Report

One of the most common mistakes our clients make is trying to use a simple Justice of the Peace (JP) affidavit to explain away a lost passport. You cannot use an affidavit in place of a police report for a lost or stolen passport.

If your passport is missing, you must visit a police station and obtain an official, stamped Police Report. This report is non-negotiable. It must clearly state your full name, the fact that your Jamaican passport was specifically lost or stolen, and ideally the passport number if you have it on record.

Pro Tip: PICA requires the police report to be recent, typically issued within the last six months. Do not try to use a police report from two years ago to replace a passport you lost back then. You must get an updated report.

Damaged Passports: The Surrender and Declaration Rule

What if your passport isn't lost, but it’s badly water-logged, torn, or the bio-data page is illegible? The rules change slightly here. You do not need a police report for a damaged passport, because you still possess the document.

However, you MUST surrender the damaged passport booklet with your application. You cannot keep it as a souvenir. Furthermore, you must provide a formal Letter of Explanation or a Statutory Declaration (Affidavit) that has been signed and stamped by a Justice of the Peace or a Notary Public. This declaration must outline the exact circumstances of how the damage occurred.

To help you save time and avoid drafting errors, we’ve provided a standard template for this declaration below:

📝 Template: Statutory Declaration for a Damaged Passport

I, [YOUR FULL NAME], of [YOUR FULL ADDRESS], being of [YOUR OCCUPATION], do solemnly and sincerely declare as follows:

1. That I am a Jamaican citizen and the holder of Jamaican Passport number [PASSPORT NUMBER] issued on [DATE OF ISSUE].
2. That on or about [DATE OF INCIDENT], the said passport was severely damaged when [INSERT DETAILED REASON, e.g., "it was accidentally left in my trousers and washed in a washing machine"].
3. That I am surrendering this damaged passport alongside my application for a replacement.

I make this solemn declaration conscientiously believing the same to be true.

Signature of Applicant: _______________________
Declared before me at: _______________________ on this ____ day of ________, 20__.
Justice of the Peace / Notary Public: _______________________ [STAMP/SEAL HERE]

Will I Pay the Same Fee as a Regular Renewal?

No. Replacing a lost, stolen, or damaged passport carries a steep penalty fee. While a standard adult passport renewal currently costs JMD $6,500, the fee to replace a lost or damaged adult passport jumps to JMD $11,500.

This higher processing fee reflects the additional administrative and investigative resources required by PICA to secure your identity. Furthermore, remember that you cannot use the online portal; replacement applications must be processed locally at a PICA office or submitted through your nearest Jamaican High Commission or Embassy if you live overseas.
